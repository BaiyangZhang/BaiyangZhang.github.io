

In a free scalar field theory, the vacuum state is uniquely specified in two ways: it is the state of minimal energy, and it is the state annihilated by the particle number operator $a_p^\dagger a_p$. When interactions, mass shifts, or topological backgrounds such as a kink are introduced, these two specifications no longer coincide and the trivial vacuum becomes polarized. In standard perturbation theory, one works in the interaction picture, where the field operator evolves via the free Hamiltonian and is tied to a single free-particle Fock space. In the presence of vacuum polarization or a non-trivial classical background, however, the time dependence of the modes changes across sectors, making the interaction picture inconsistent. We therefore work exclusively in the Schrödinger picture, where any operator $\mathcal{O}(\phi(\vec{x}), \pi(\vec{x}))$ is a time-independent functional of the spatial field $\phi(\vec{x})$ and its canonical momentum density $\pi(\vec{x})$.

  

We consider a real scalar field in $d+1$ dimensions quantized via the equal-time canonical commutation relation \begin{equation} [\phi(\vec{x}), \pi(\vec{y})] = i\delta^{(d)}(\vec{x}-\vec{y}), \end{equation} with all other equal-time commutators vanishing. This fundamental quantization relation holds across the entire theory and is unaffected by the choice of sector. Because the theory is quantized only once in physical space, all sectors share the same underlying operator algebra and ultraviolet regularization.

  

In the trivial or unbroken vacuum sector, the linearized classical equations of motion are solved by plane waves of frequency $\omega_p = \sqrt{\vec{p}^2+m^2}$. We decompose the Schrödinger field and momentum operators at a reference mass scale $m$ as \begin{equation} \phi(\vec{x}) = \int \frac{d^d p}{(2\pi)^d} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_p}} \left(a_p e^{i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}} + a_p^\dagger e^{-i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}}\right), \end{equation} where $[a_p, a_k^\dagger] = (2\pi)^d \delta^{(d)}(\vec{p}-\vec{k})$. To avoid square roots of frequencies in multi-meson states, we introduce the rescaled ladder operators \begin{equation} A_p \equiv \sqrt{2\omega_p},a_p, \quad A_p^\dagger \equiv \sqrt{2\omega_p},a_p^\dagger, \quad A_p^\ddagger := \frac{A_p^\dagger}{2\omega_p} = \frac{a_p^\dagger}{\sqrt{2\omega_p}}, \end{equation} which satisfy the canonical commutation relation \begin{equation} [A_p, A_k^\ddagger] = (2\pi)^d \delta^{(d)}(\vec{p}-\vec{k}). \end{equation} In terms of $A_p$ and $A_p^\ddagger$, the plane-wave decomposition takes the form \begin{align} \phi(\vec{x}) &= \int \frac{d^d p}{(2\pi)^d} , e^{-i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}} \left(A_p^\ddagger + \frac{A_{-p}}{2\omega_p}\right) = \phi^+(\vec{x}) + \phi^-(\vec{x}), \nonumber \ \pi(\vec{x}) &= i \int \frac{d^d p}{(2\pi)^d} , e^{-i\vec{p}\cdot\vec{x}} \left(\omega_p A_p^\ddagger - \frac{A_{-p}}{2}\right) = \pi^+(\vec{x}) + \pi^-(\vec{x}), \end{align} where $\phi^+(\vec{x})$ and $\pi^+(\vec{x})$ contain only creation operators $A_p^\ddagger$, while $\phi^-(\vec{x})$ and $\pi^-(\vec{x})$ contain only annihilation operators $A_p$. Plane-wave normal ordering $:\mathcal{O}:_m$ at mass scale $m$ places all $A_{p,m}^\ddagger$ to the left of all $A_{p,m}$. While the Schrödinger operators $\phi(\vec{x})$ and $\pi(\vec{x})$ are independent of the mass parameter $m$, the ladder operators $A_{p,m}, A_{p,m}^\ddagger$, the frequencies $\omega_{p,m}$, and the Fock states built upon the free vacuum increase or shift when the normal-ordering mass scale is changed.

  

When we study a state $|\Psi\rangle$ with a non-vanishing field expectation value $\langle\Psi|\phi(\vec{x})|\Psi\rangle = f(\vec{x})$—such as a spontaneously broken vacuum $|0^+\rangle$ with $\langle 0^+|\phi(\vec{x})|0^+\rangle = v_0 = \frac{m_0}{\sqrt{2\lambda_0}}$ or a soliton state $|K\rangle$ with classical profile $f(\vec{x})$—a direct perturbative expansion in moments of $\phi(\vec{x})$ fails. In weak-coupling perturbation theory, $f(\vec{x})$ scales as $\mathcal{O}(1/g)$, where $g = \sqrt{\lambda}$, which diverges as $g \to 0$. In the path integral formalism, one handles this by shifting the integration variable $\phi \to f + \phi'$ so that the fluctuation field $\phi'$ is centered at zero. In the Hamiltonian formalism, we need an analogous way to find a basic quantum field operator whose expectation value vanishes in the state $|\Psi\rangle$.

  

This is achieved from a passive perspective using the unitary displacement operator \begin{equation} \mathcal{D}f := \exp\left{-i\pi(f)\right}, \quad \pi(f) := \int d^d x , f(\vec{x})\pi(\vec{x}), \end{equation} where $\pi(f)$ is the smeared canonical momentum operator. Just as the spatial translation operator $T{\vec{x}} = \exp(-i\hat{P}\cdot\vec{x})$ shifts coordinates in configuration space via $T_{\vec{x}}^\dagger \phi(\vec{y}) T_{\vec{x}} = \phi(\vec{y}+\vec{x})$, the displacement operator shifts the field operator in function space. Using the canonical commutation relation, we have \begin{equation} [\phi(\vec{x}), \mathcal{D}_f] = e^{-i\pi(f)} [\phi(\vec{x}), -i\pi(f)] = f(\vec{x})\mathcal{D}_f, \end{equation} which yields the unitary transformations \begin{equation} \mathcal{D}_f \phi(\vec{x}) \mathcal{D}_f^\dagger = \phi(\vec{x}) - f(\vec{x}), \quad \mathcal{D}_f^\dagger \phi(\vec{x}) \mathcal{D}_f = \phi(\vec{x}) + f(\vec{x}). \end{equation}

  

In an active perspective, $\mathcal{D}_f|\Psi\rangle$ is viewed as a new physical state displaced in a fixed coordinate system. In our passive perspective, the physical state $|\Psi\rangle$ and the defining Hamiltonian $\hat{H}[\phi, \pi]$ remain completely unchanged; instead, $\mathcal{D}_f$ shifts the origin of the field-space coordinate system. This is identical to studying a regular function $f(x)$ near a point $x_0$ far from the origin: because $x_0$ is not small, a Maclaurin expansion around $x=0$ fails, so we passively shift the coordinate origin to $x_0$ and expand the same function in small deviations $\bar{x} = x - x_0$. In the kink sector, where $\langle K|\phi(\vec{x})|K\rangle = f(\vec{x})$ at leading order, we define the perturbative kink field operator $\varphi(\vec{x})$ as \begin{equation} \varphi(\vec{x}) := \mathcal{D}_f \phi(\vec{x}) \mathcal{D}_f^\dagger = \phi(\vec{x}) - f(\vec{x}), \end{equation} so that its leading expectation value vanishes in the kink state: \begin{equation} \langle K|\varphi(\vec{x})|K\rangle = \langle K|\phi(\vec{x}) - f(\vec{x})|K\rangle = 0. \end{equation} Note that $\varphi(\vec{x})$ satisfies the same canonical commutation relation with $\pi(\vec{y})$ and the same commutation relation with $\mathcal{D}_f$ as $\phi(\vec{x})$, namely $[\varphi(\vec{x}), \mathcal{D}_f] = f(\vec{x})\mathcal{D}_f$. Inverting the definition gives $\phi(\vec{x}) = \mathcal{D}_f^\dagger \varphi(\vec{x}) \mathcal{D}_f = \varphi(\vec{x}) + f(\vec{x})$, which allows us to rewrite the original Hamiltonian $\hat{H}[\phi, \pi]$ in terms of the perturbative field $\varphi(\vec{x})$: \begin{equation} \hat{H}[\phi, \pi] = \hat{H}[\mathcal{D}_f^\dagger \varphi \mathcal{D}_f, \pi] = \mathcal{D}_f^\dagger \hat{H}[\varphi, \pi] \mathcal{D}_f = \hat{H}[\varphi + f, \pi] =: H'[\varphi, \pi]. \end{equation} Because $H'[\varphi, \pi]$ is the exact same operator as $\hat{H}[\phi, \pi]$, merely expressed in terms of $\varphi(\vec{x})$, we can apply perturbation theory directly to $H'[\varphi, \pi]$ acting on $|K\rangle$ without altering the eigenvalue spectrum or introducing regulator ambiguities between sectors.

  

Once the Hamiltonian is rewritten in terms of the perturbative field of a given sector, a crucial question arises: in what basis of modes should the perturbative field operator be expanded? In general, if an operator $\mathcal{O}$ is diagonalized in a basis of states, its shifted expression in terms of a displaced field is no longer diagonal in that same basis unless the shift is spatially homogeneous.

  

Consider first the broken-vacuum sector $|0^+\rangle$, where the classical background is a spatial constant $f(\vec{x}) = v$ (with $v = \frac{m}{\sqrt{2\lambda}} + \delta v$ in the $\phi^4$ model). Defining the shifted vacuum field $\phi'(\vec{x}) := \mathcal{D}_{-v}\phi(\vec{x})\mathcal{D}_{-v}^\dagger = \phi(\vec{x}) - v$ so that $\langle 0^+|\phi'(\vec{x})|0^+\rangle = 0$, we rewrite the Hamiltonian as $H[\phi', \pi] = \hat{H}[\phi'+v, \pi]$. Because $v$ is a constant $c$-number, the quadratic part $H_2[\phi', \pi]$ has a constant mass squared $m^2$ and remains diagonal in the plane-wave basis $\{A_p^\ddagger, A_p\}$. The leading-order broken vacuum $|\Omega_0\rangle$ therefore satisfies $A_p|\Omega_0\rangle = 0$.

  

Now contrast this with the soliton sector, where $f(x)$ is a spatially varying kink or domain wall profile solving the static classical equation of motion $\partial_x^2 f(x) = V^{(1)}(\sqrt{\lambda}f(x))/\sqrt{\lambda}$. When we expand the rewritten Hamiltonian $H'[\varphi, \pi] = \hat{H}[\varphi+f, \pi]$ in powers of $\varphi$, the free quadratic part takes the form \begin{equation} H'_2[\varphi, \pi] = \frac{1}{2} \int d^d x \left( :\pi^2(\vec{x}):_m + :(\vec{\nabla}\varphi(\vec{x}))^2:_m + V^{(2)}(\sqrt{\lambda}f(x)):\varphi^2(\vec{x}):_m \right). \end{equation} Because the effective mass squared $V^{(2)}(\sqrt{\lambda}f(x))$ depends explicitly on position $x$, substituting the plane-wave decomposition $\{A_p^\ddagger, A_p\}$ into $H'_2[\varphi, \pi]$ produces off-diagonal $A^\ddagger A^\ddagger$ and $AA$ terms that fail to diagonalize $H'_2$. Instead, small constant-frequency fluctuations $\varphi(x,t) = e^{-i\omega t}\mathfrak{g}(x)$ about the kink satisfy the Sturm-Liouville eigenvalue equation \begin{equation} \left(-\partial_x^2 + V^{(2)}(\sqrt{\lambda}f(x))\right)\mathfrak{g}_k(x) = \omega_k^2 \mathfrak{g}_k(x). \end{equation} The solutions $\mathfrak{g}_k(x)$ form a complete orthonormal basis of functions, comprising three distinct classes of normal modes:

  

1. A real translational zero mode $\mathfrak{g}_B(x) = -f'(x)/\sqrt{Q_0}$ with frequency $\omega_B = 0$, where $Q_0$ is the classical kink energy.
    
      
    
2. Discrete bound shape modes $\mathfrak{g}_S(x)$ with $0 < \omega_S < m$.
    
      
    
3. Continuum meson modes $\mathfrak{g}_k(x)$ with $\omega_k = \sqrt{m^2+k^2}$.
    
      
    

Normalized to satisfy $\int dx\, |\mathfrak{g}_B(x)|^2 = 1$, $\int dx\, \mathfrak{g}_{S_1}(x)\mathfrak{g}_{S_2}^*(x) = \delta_{S_1 S_2}$, and $\int dx\, \mathfrak{g}_{k_1}(x)\mathfrak{g}_{k_2}^*(x) = 2\pi\delta(k_1-k_2)$ with $\mathfrak{g}_k^* = \mathfrak{g}_{-k}$, these modes obey the completeness relation \begin{equation} \mathfrak{g}_B(x)\mathfrak{g}_B(y) + \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} , \mathfrak{g}_k(x)\mathfrak{g}_{-k}(y) = \delta(x-y), \quad \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} := \sum_S + \int \frac{dk}{2\pi}. \end{equation} For a planar domain wall in $d$ spatial dimensions, the normal modes factorize into the $1+1$ dimensional kink normal modes in the $x$ direction and plane waves in the $d-1$ transverse directions $\vec{y}$, $\mathfrak{g}_{\vec{k}}(\vec{x}) = \mathfrak{g}_{k_x}(x)e^{-i\vec{k}_y\cdot\vec{y}}$, with frequencies $\omega_{B\vec{k}_y} = \vert{}\vec{k}_y\vert{}$, $\omega_{S\vec{k}_y} = \sqrt{\omega_S^2+\vec{k}_y^2}$, and $\omega_{k_x\vec{k}_y} = \sqrt{m^2+k_x^2+\vec{k}_y^2}$.

  

In the 1+1 dimensional kink sector, we must expand the kink perturbative field $\varphi(x)$ and conjugate momentum $\pi(x)$ in terms of the Sturm-Liouville normal modes as \begin{align} \varphi(x) &= \varphi_0 \mathfrak{g}_B(x) + \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} \left(B_k^\ddagger + \frac{B_{-k}}{2\omega_k}\right) \mathfrak{g}k(x), \nonumber \ \pi(x) &= \pi_0 \mathfrak{g}_B(x) + i \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} \left(\omega_k B_k^\ddagger - \frac{B_{-k}}{2}\right) \mathfrak{g}_k(x), \end{align} where $B_k^\ddagger = B_k^\dagger/(2\omega_k)$ and $B_{-S} = B_S$. Using the equal-time commutator $[\varphi(x), \pi(y)] = i\delta(x-y)$, the normal-mode operators satisfy the canonical algebra \begin{equation} [\varphi_0, \pi_0] = i, \quad [B{S_1}, B_{S_2}^\ddagger] = \delta_{S_1 S_2}, \quad [B_{k_1}, B_{k_2}^\ddagger] = 2\pi\delta(k_1-k_2). \end{equation} Here $\varphi_0/\sqrt{Q_0}$ is the linear approximation to the collective coordinate $x_0$ of the kink center of mass, and $\pi_0$ is proportional to the kink center-of-mass momentum. Substituting this normal-mode expansion into $H'_2[\varphi, \pi]$ diagonalizes the free kink Hamiltonian: \begin{equation} H'_2 = Q_1 + \frac{\pi_0^2}{2} + \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} , \omega_k B_k^\ddagger B_k, \end{equation} where $Q_1$ is the one-loop kink mass correction arising from reordering the plane-wave normal-ordered operators into normal-mode normal-ordered form ($::b$, which places $\varphi_0$ and $B_k^\ddagger$ to the left of $\pi_0$ and $B_k$). The leading-order kink ground state $\vert{}K_0\rangle$ is therefore defined by the conditions \begin{equation} \pi_0|K_0\rangle = B_S|K_0\rangle = B_k|K_0\rangle = 0. \end{equation} Because the plane-wave ladder operators $\{A_p^\ddagger, A_p\}$ and the normal-mode operators $\{\varphi_0, \pi_0, B_k^\ddagger, B_k\}$ are related by a Bogoliubov transformation, \begin{align} A_p^\ddagger &= \tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_B(-p)\left(\frac{\varphi_0}{2} - \frac{i\pi_0}{2\omega_p}\right) + \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} , \tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_k(-p) \left(\frac{\omega_k+\omega_p}{2\omega_p}B_k^\ddagger + \frac{\omega_p-\omega_k}{4\omega_k\omega_p}B_{-k}\right), \nonumber \ A_{-p} &= \tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_B(-p)\left(\omega_p\varphi_0 + i\pi_0\right) + \sum!!!!!!!\int \frac{dk}{2\pi} , \tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_k(-p) \left((\omega_p-\omega_k)B_k^\ddagger + \frac{\omega_p+\omega_k}{2\omega_k}B_{-k}\right), \end{align} where $\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}(p) = \int dx\,\mathfrak{g}(x)e^{-ipx}$, the soliton ground state is a squeezed coherent state rather than an ordinary coherent state.

  

This highlights a strict structural rule in our framework: there is a one-to-one correspondence between the perturbative field operator of a given sector and the basis of eigenmodes in which that operator must be expanded. In the broken-vacuum sector, the perturbative field $\phi'(\vec{x}) = \phi(\vec{x}) - v$ must be expanded in plane waves $\{A_p^\ddagger, A_p\}$, which diagonalize $H_2[\phi', \pi]$ and annihilate the leading vacuum $\vert{}\Omega_0\rangle$. In the soliton sector, the perturbative kink field $\varphi(\vec{x}) = \phi(\vec{x}) - f(\vec{x})$ must be expanded in the Sturm-Liouville normal modes $\{\varphi_0, \pi_0, B_k^\ddagger, B_k\}$, which diagonalize $H'_2[\varphi, \pi]$ and annihilate the leading kink state $\vert{}K_0\rangle$. A mismatch between the perturbative field operator and its sector-specific mode expansion—such as expanding the kink field $\varphi(\vec{x})$ in plane waves or acting on the kink ground state with un-squeezed plane-wave operators—leaves off-diagonal quadratic terms in the free Hamiltonian, misidentifies the leading ground state, and produces severe computational errors, including unphysical ultraviolet divergences in the energy density.
